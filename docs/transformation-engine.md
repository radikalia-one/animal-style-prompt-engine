# Transformation Engine Specification

## 1. Purpose

The Transformation Engine is the system responsible for combining Animal DNA and Style DNA into a coherent transformation strategy.

Its purpose is to answer:

"How should this specific animal be translated into this specific visual style without losing its identity?"

The engine must preserve recognizable animal characteristics while applying the reusable design language extracted from the style reference.

---

## 2. Inputs

The Transformation Engine receives two structured inputs:

### Animal DNA

Defines:

- Species.
- Silhouette.
- Anatomy.
- Signature features.
- Expression.
- Pose.
- Natural colors.
- Markings.
- Identity priorities.

### Style DNA

Defines:

- Shape language.
- Volume hierarchy.
- Proportion philosophy.
- Color system.
- Material system.
- Surface treatment.
- Construction logic.
- Detail density.
- Lighting.
- Rendering.
- Composition.
- Negative rules.

---

## 3. Main Transformation Principle

The system must not replace Animal DNA with Style DNA.

Instead:

Animal DNA determines WHAT must remain recognizable.

Style DNA determines HOW those features are visually represented.

The Transformation Engine translates one into the other.

---

## 4. Translation Hierarchy

The engine should process features in this order:

1. Species identity.
2. Signature silhouette.
3. Essential anatomy.
4. Signature features.
5. Facial identity.
6. Pose.
7. Expression.
8. Style shape language.
9. Style materials.
10. Style color system.
11. Surface treatment.
12. Construction details.
13. Lighting.
14. Rendering.
15. Composition.

Higher-priority identity features override lower-priority stylistic choices when conflicts occur.

---

## 5. Immutable Features

Animal DNA Tier A features are considered immutable.

Examples:

- Elephant trunk.
- Giraffe neck.
- Toucan beak.
- Rhinoceros beetle horn.
- Tree frog adhesive toe pads.

The style may simplify or reconstruct these features, but must not remove or replace them.

Example:

Animal feature:
Toucan oversized beak

Style:
Minimal vinyl toy

Translation:
Oversized smooth vinyl beak volume with simplified color blocking.

The beak remains recognizable.

---

## 6. Flexible Features

Animal DNA Tier C features may be heavily transformed.

Examples:

- Fine fur strands.
- Small wrinkles.
- Tiny scales.
- Minor feather detail.
- Skin pores.

These may become:

- Smooth surfaces.
- Panels.
- Painted shapes.
- Shallow relief.
- Simplified sculptural volumes.

---

## 7. Shape Translation

The engine should translate natural anatomical forms into the shape language defined by Style DNA.

Examples:

Natural:
Muscular torso

Style:
Rounded designer toy

Translation:
Large compact rounded torso volume.

---

Natural:
Sharp bird beak

Style:
Soft inflated sculpture

Translation:
Smooth tapered beak with softened transitions while preserving recognizable geometry.

---

Natural:
Segmented insect abdomen

Style:
Minimal sofubi toy

Translation:
Large unified body mass with restrained segmentation.

---

## 8. Surface Translation

Natural biological surfaces should be translated according to the selected style.

Examples:

Fur
→ smooth sculptural surface
→ shallow grooves
→ large graphic color blocks
→ soft molded relief

Feathers
→ layered panels
→ sculpted overlapping masses
→ simplified wing plates

Scales
→ shallow segmentation
→ embossed patterns
→ smooth molded surfaces

Skin
→ satin polymer
→ vinyl
→ clay
→ ceramic
→ painted resin

Exoskeleton
→ molded shell
→ hard polymer plates
→ simplified armored volumes

The translation must follow Style DNA.

---

## 9. Color Translation

The engine must combine:

Animal identity colors

with

Style color philosophy.

The system should determine which colors are identity-critical.

Example:

Red-eyed tree frog:

Identity colors:

- Green body.
- Red eyes.
- Orange feet.

Style DNA:

- Two dominant colors.
- One accent.
- Dark neutral structural color.

Possible translation:

- Green primary shell.
- Dark charcoal secondary regions.
- Red eye accents.
- Orange toe-pad accents.

Colors should be simplified without destroying recognition.

---

## 10. Marking Translation

Natural markings may become:

- Painted masks.
- Graphic panels.
- Color blocks.
- Sculpted relief.
- Material changes.
- Recessed shapes.

Examples:

Tiger stripes
→ dark graphic panels following body flow.

Leopard spots
→ controlled circular painted modules.

Zebra pattern
→ large simplified black-and-white segmentation.

Bird plumage pattern
→ layered color zones.

Do not apply markings randomly.

---

## 11. Facial Translation

The face requires special protection.

The engine must preserve:

- Eye spacing.
- Eye orientation.
- Muzzle or beak relationship.
- Signature expression.
- Species-specific head structure.

Style DNA may change:

- Eye material.
- Surface material.
- Shape simplification.
- Color framing.
- Detail level.

Example:

Animal:
Tree frog with protruding red eyes.

Style:
Mechanical toy.

Translation:
Large protruding optical lens modules with red accent housings preserving original spacing and orientation.

---

## 12. Limb Translation

The engine should preserve:

- Number of limbs.
- Relative length.
- Functional anatomy.
- Characteristic stance.

Style DNA determines their construction.

Examples:

Natural leg
→ smooth vinyl limb.

Natural paw
→ molded rubber pad.

Bird talon
→ simplified curved polymer claw.

Insect leg
→ segmented sculptural appendage.

Never replace species-specific limbs with humanoid arms or legs unless explicitly requested.

---

## 13. Material Translation

Style DNA defines final materials.

Animal DNA determines where those materials should respect anatomical identity.

Example:

Animal:
Frog.

Style material system:

- Satin polymer shell.
- Rubber joints.
- Glossy optical eyes.

Translation:

Body
→ satin polymer.

Toe pads
→ soft rubber.

Eyes
→ glossy optical lenses.

This creates a coherent material system.

---

## 14. Proportion Translation

The system may stylize proportions only within acceptable limits.

Allowed:

- Slightly enlarge eyes.
- Compact body.
- Simplify limb thickness.
- Increase toy-like head size.

Not allowed when identity is harmed:

- Remove long giraffe neck.
- Shorten elephant trunk beyond recognition.
- Remove insect horn.
- Give owl sideways eyes.
- Replace frog legs with mammalian legs.

Species identity has priority over stylization.

---

## 15. Construction Translation

The engine should interpret Style DNA construction rules.

Possible outputs include:

- One-piece sculpture.
- Soft vinyl toy.
- Injection-molded shell.
- Mechanical assembly.
- Clay sculpture.
- Ceramic figure.
- Layered paper object.

Anatomical divisions may become construction divisions.

Examples:

Shoulder
→ panel boundary.

Wing
→ separate shell volume.

Horn
→ molded insert.

Tail
→ articulated component.

Construction must feel intentional.

---

## 16. Detail Allocation

The engine should preserve visual hierarchy.

Suggested strategy:

Primary forms:
high importance

Secondary anatomy:
medium importance

Tertiary biological detail:
low importance

Style details should be concentrated around:

- Face.
- Signature features.
- Major anatomical transitions.

Avoid uniform visual noise.

---

## 17. Conflict Resolution

When Animal DNA and Style DNA conflict, use the following rules.

### Conflict Example 1

Animal DNA:
Very long ears.

Style DNA:
Compact proportions.

Resolution:
Preserve long ears but simplify their shape and thickness.

---

### Conflict Example 2

Animal DNA:
Dense fur.

Style DNA:
Smooth vinyl.

Resolution:
Remove literal fur texture but preserve fur volume and color pattern.

---

### Conflict Example 3

Animal DNA:
Bright natural coloration.

Style DNA:
Muted palette.

Resolution:
Reduce saturation while preserving the relative identity colors.

---

### Conflict Example 4

Animal DNA:
Sharp horn.

Style DNA:
Soft rounded geometry.

Resolution:
Preserve horn silhouette while softening surface transitions and tip sharpness.

---

## 18. Transformation Strength

Future versions may support a transformation strength control.

Example:

### Low Stylization

- Preserve most anatomy.
- Preserve natural proportions.
- Apply mainly materials and rendering.

### Medium Stylization

- Simplify anatomy.
- Modify proportions moderately.
- Apply stronger shape language.

### High Stylization

- Strong design transformation.
- Aggressive simplification.
- Strong style proportions.
- Still preserve Tier A identity features.

Species identity must survive all levels.

---

## 19. Originality Rules

The Transformation Engine must not copy:

- Exact reference character.
- Unique accessories.
- Logos.
- Brand-specific details.
- Exact decorative patterns.
- Character-specific features unrelated to the style.

The engine may reuse only general visual principles.

---

## 20. Mutation Prevention

The engine must actively prevent:

- Extra limbs.
- Missing limbs.
- Wrong species features.
- Incorrect wing type.
- Incorrect tail structure.
- Incorrect beak.
- Incorrect horn placement.
- Humanoid anatomy.
- Generic robotic replacement.
- Random accessories.
- Uncontrolled mutations.

---

## 22. Transformation Output Example

Animal DNA:

- Red-eyed tree frog.
- Bright green body.
- Large protruding red eyes.
- Long thin limbs.
- Orange adhesive toe pads.

Style DNA:

- Premium mechanical designer toy.
- Rounded polymer forms.
- Charcoal structural panels.
- Glossy optical lenses.
- Rubberized joints.
- Controlled orange accents.

Transformation Output:

- Preserve compact frog silhouette.
- Rebuild body as smooth green polymer shell.
- Convert red eyes into oversized glossy optical lenses with red frames.
- Preserve long limbs using smooth articulated polymer forms.
- Convert adhesive toe pads into rounded orange rubber modules.
- Add restrained charcoal structural areas at limb joints.
- Avoid biological skin texture.
