# Soft-Mechanical Designer Toy — Style Analyzer

## Purpose

This analyzer extracts the reusable visual language of a premium soft-mechanical designer-toy style from one or more reference images.

The analyzer must never copy the specific reference character.

Its job is to identify the design principles that can be transferred to any animal.

---

## 1. Analyze Shape Language

Identify:

- Primary body volumes.
- Secondary structural forms.
- Rounded versus angular geometry.
- Edge radius.
- Surface continuity.
- Compactness.
- Inflation.
- Tapering.
- Symmetry.
- Repeated geometric motifs.

Determine whether the design uses:

- Large rounded primary masses.
- Minimal secondary forms.
- Restrained tertiary detail.
- Soft transitions.
- Precision manufactured geometry.

---

## 2. Analyze Mechanical Language

Identify:

- Panel segmentation.
- Recessed seams.
- Rounded-cone modules.
- Optical components.
- Soft mechanical joints.
- Vent shapes.
- Insert geometry.
- Structural housings.
- Functional-looking decorative elements.

Mechanical detail must remain restrained.

Avoid interpreting the style as:

- Heavy machinery.
- Military robotics.
- Cyberpunk armor.
- Industrial machinery.
- Exposed mechanical skeleton.

The style should feel soft, friendly, compact, and manufacturable.

---

## 3. Analyze Material System

Identify apparent materials such as:

- Satin injection-molded polymer.
- Matte soft-touch elastomer.
- Dense rubber.
- Glossy smoked optical glass.
- Painted plastic.
- Dark polymer structural components.
- Restrained metallic accents.

For each material determine:

- Gloss.
- Roughness.
- Reflectivity.
- Surface softness.
- Color role.
- Placement.

---

## 4. Analyze Color Philosophy

Identify:

- Primary shell color.
- Secondary shell color.
- Dark structural color.
- Accent colors.
- Neutral details.

Determine:

- Number of major colors.
- Percentage of each color.
- Accent placement.
- Contrast level.
- Saturation.
- Whether colors follow anatomy or construction.

Color must be organized deliberately.

---

## 5. Analyze Eye Construction

Study:

- Eye size.
- Eye spacing.
- Eye orientation.
- Lens shape.
- Outer frame.
- Accent housing.
- Reflection behavior.
- Depth.
- Gloss.

The eye system may become a major focal point.

Do not force identical eyes onto every species.

The final design must adapt the optical construction to the anatomy of the uploaded animal.

---

## 6. Analyze Facial Construction

Study:

- Muzzle.
- Nose.
- Beak.
- Mouth.
- Jaw.
- Brow.
- Eye framing.
- Facial panel flow.

Determine how biological facial anatomy is translated into:

- Polymer volumes.
- Rubber components.
- Optical modules.
- Clean panel divisions.

Species identity must remain recognizable.

---

## 7. Analyze Surface Simplification

Determine how natural texture is removed or translated.

Examples:

Fur
→ smooth sculptural shell.

Feathers
→ molded layered panels.

Scales
→ shallow geometric segmentation.

Skin
→ clean polymer surface.

Exoskeleton
→ simplified shell plates.

Natural textures must not remain literal unless the reference style clearly requires them.

---

## 8. Analyze Detail Density

Determine:

- Where details are concentrated.
- Where large clean areas remain.
- Frequency of seams.
- Frequency of modules.
- Relationship between primary and tertiary details.

The style should not become visually noisy.

---

## 9. Analyze Construction Logic

Determine whether the character appears:

- Injection molded.
- Modular.
- Soft robotic.
- Collectible.
- Industrially manufactured.

Identify:

- Hidden fasteners.
- Molded joints.
- Recessed seams.
- Separate shell parts.
- Inserted modules.

Construction details should appear plausible but simplified.

---

## 10. Analyze Proportion Philosophy

Determine:

- Head-to-body ratio.
- Eye scale.
- Limb thickness.
- Body compactness.
- Degree of exaggeration.
- Toy-like simplification.

The analyzer must separate:

Style proportions

from

species-specific proportions.

The final result should not force every animal into the same body shape.

---

## 11. Analyze Lighting

Identify:

- Key-light direction.
- Light softness.
- Highlight size.
- Fill intensity.
- Rim lighting.
- Contact shadows.
- Ambient occlusion.
- Reflection strength.

The lighting should support product visualization.

---

## 12. Analyze Rendering Style

Determine:

- CGI quality.
- Product-render influence.
- Realism level.
- Stylization level.
- Reflection quality.
- Shadow softness.
- Edge cleanliness.
- Background simplicity.

---

## 13. Analyze Background and Composition

Identify:

- Camera angle.
- Subject rotation.
- Framing.
- Eye level.
- Lens feel.
- Background gradient.
- Empty space.
- Product-shot presentation.

---

## 14. Extract Reusable Style DNA

Return the analysis using these categories:

- Style Summary
- Shape Language
- Volume Hierarchy
- Proportion Philosophy
- Mechanical Language
- Material System
- Color System
- Eye Construction
- Facial Construction
- Surface Translation
- Construction Logic
- Detail Density
- Lighting
- Rendering
- Composition
- Negative Rules

Do not describe the reference subject unless necessary to explain a transferable rule.

---

## 15. Originality Rule

Never copy:

- The exact reference character.
- Specific accessories.
- Logos.
- Character-specific patterns.
- Unique decorative arrangements.
- Exact anatomical mutations belonging only to the reference.

Extract only reusable visual principles.

---

## 16. Final Goal

The extracted Style DNA must be general enough to transform:

- Mammals.
- Birds.
- Reptiles.
- Amphibians.
- Fish.
- Insects.
- Marine animals.

The final design must feel like part of the same visual design family while remaining an original representation of the uploaded animal.
